Genetic clusters of type 1 diabetes

People with type 1 diabetes don’t all carry the same genetic risk. Using T1GRS, our machine-learning genetic risk score, we grouped people by which risk variants drive their score and found four genetic clusters with different ages of onset and rates of complications. This site lets you explore those clusters.

From McGrail, Sears, Griffin et al., Nature Genetics (2026)

About T1GRS

T1GRS is a genetic risk score for type 1 diabetes built with machine learning. Instead of adding up the effect of each risk allele, as earlier scores do, it uses a gradient-boosted model that can learn how variants act together. It considers 199 variants: 97 in the HLA region, which carries the largest share of inherited risk, and 102 at risk loci elsewhere in the genome.

The variants come from a genome-wide association and fine-mapping study of 20,355 people with type 1 diabetes and 797,363 without it, which identified 160 risk signals. Compared with the widely used GRS2 score, T1GRS predicted type 1 diabetes more accurately, with the largest gains in people who lack the high-risk HLA-DR3 and DR4 haplotypes. The model also revealed 154 significant interactions between variants, including between HLA and non-HLA loci such as INS.

What a cluster means here

A cluster is a group of people whose T1GRS predictions are driven by similar genetic factors. Two people can have the same overall score for different reasons, and the clusters capture those reasons. They describe patterns of inherited risk, are not diagnoses, and should not be used to make decisions about individual care.

The four clusters

Each cluster is named for the genetic factors that matter most to its members’ scores. The cluster numbers used in the paper are shown in case your tables use them.

MHC-driven Cluster 4
Risk is dominated by HLA class II alleles. Along with the MHC-enriched cluster, onset tends to come several years earlier than in the other clusters.
MHC-enriched Cluster 2
The MHC region still contributes strongly, with HLA class I alleles mattering most. Risk variants are enriched in pancreatic regulatory elements.
T cell-enriched Cluster 1
The PTPN22 locus is the most important feature, and the key variants fall in regulatory elements active in T cells.
Pancreas-enriched Cluster 3
The INS locus matters most and key variants sit in pancreatic regulatory elements. This cluster has the lowest average T1GRS and the latest onset, yet higher rates of nephropathy, neuropathy and cardiovascular disease, a pattern replicated in the All of Us cohort.

How the clusters were defined

  1. Genetic discovery

    Association and fine-mapping in over 817,000 people of European ancestry identified 160 type 1 diabetes risk signals, including detailed mapping of the HLA region.

  2. Training T1GRS

    A CatBoost model was trained on 199 risk variants in 29,746 people (10,107 with type 1 diabetes) and checked with tenfold cross-validation and independent cohorts.

  3. Genetic profiles

    For every person, SHAP values measure how much each variant pushed their T1GRS prediction up or down. Together these form their genetic profile.

  4. Clustering

    Profiles were reduced with PCA, linked in a nearest-neighbour graph and grouped with Leiden clustering, then displayed with UMAP. People from All of Us were projected onto the clusters to validate them.
